# Prior-art ledger

Last searched: 2026-08-22. This is a living ledger, not proof of novelty.

## Established results

1. Dempster et al., *Agreement between two large pan-cancer CRISPR-Cas9 gene
   dependency data sets*, Nature Communications (2019).
   <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31862961/>
   Overall Broad–Sanger agreement and major library/duration batch effects are known.

2. Pacini et al., *Integrated cross-study datasets of genetic dependencies in
   cancer*, Nature Communications (2021).
   <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33712601/>
   Cross-study harmonization and integration are known; integration itself is not new.

3. Behan et al., *Prioritization of cancer therapeutic targets using CRISPR-Cas9
   screens*, Nature (2019).
   <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30971826/>
   Project Score and MSI-selective WRN dependency provide a positive control.

4. Dede and Hart, *Recovering false negatives in CRISPR fitness screens with JLOE*,
   Nucleic Acids Research (2023).
   <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36727483/>
   False negatives and expression-dependent replicability are known concerns.

5. Gonçalves et al., *A benchmark of computational methods for correcting biases of
   established and unknown origin in CRISPR-Cas9 screening data* (2024).
   <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39030569/>
   Copy-number and proximity-bias correction must be treated as core controls.

## Plausibly open claim

The selected gap is not overall reproducibility. It is whether rare, predefined
biomarker or lineage effects satisfy a minimum practical effect and uncertainty
threshold under screen-family holdout, without integration hiding instability.

Before any candidate is called new, search its exact biomarker–dependency pair in
PubMed, Google Scholar, DepMap/Project Score resources, Open Targets, and relevant
synthetic-lethality databases; record queries and closest matches here.
